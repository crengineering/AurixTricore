# BMP581.md — Bosch BMP581 barometer, wiring & bring-up

Wiring and bring-up notes for the **BMP581** on **I2C0**, replacing the BMP388
(CJMCU-388) and the MPU-6050 (GY-521), both removed 2026-07-31.

> **Pin-allocation SSoT:** the authoritative MCU-side pin map is
> **`PINNING.md` §2.3** (I2C0 SCL/SDA) and **§2.5** (electrical). This file
> covers the *breakout side* and does not allocate MCU pins.

Driver: `src/bsw/Bmp581.{c,h}` — written earlier, **never run against hardware**
and every register marked `TODO(hw)`. See §5 before trusting a single value.

---

## 1. Wiring — four wires, no new MCU pins

The BMP581 joins the same two pins the BMP388 used. Nothing on the MCU side
changes, so `I2c.c` needs no edit at all.

```
   +3V3 rail (X702·78/80 or X703·70) ─────────── VDD / VDDIO
   GND (X702·1-4 etc.)               ─────────── GND
   TC399 P13.1  SCL (X702·29)        ─────────── SCK / SCL
   TC399 P13.2  SDA (X702·35)        ─────────── SDI / SDA
   +3V3                              ─────────── CS      (selects I2C, §3)
   GND or +3V3                       ─────────── SDO     (address, §4)
```

| Breakout pad | Wire to | Header · pin | iLLD object |
|---|---|---|---|
| VDD / VIN | **+3V3** (see §2) | X702·78 or ·80, X703·70 | — |
| GND | GND | X702·1–4, 37, 38 … | — |
| SCK / SCL | **P13.1** | X702·**29** | `IfxI2c0_SCL_P13_1_INOUT` |
| SDI / SDA | **P13.2** | X702·**35** | `IfxI2c0_SDA_P13_2_INOUT` |
| CS | **+3V3** | — | interface select, §3 |
| SDO | GND → `0x46`, +3V3 → `0x47` | — | address select, §4 |
| INT | leave open | — | polled at 50 Hz |

---

## 2. Power — +3V3, not 5 V

Feed the breakout from the board's **regulated +3V3 rail**, not `VCC_IN`
(3.5–40 V) and not `V_UC` (5 V here). On these breakouts the SDA/SCL pull-ups
reference the module's own regulated rail, so powering it at 3.3 V keeps the
whole bus at a clean 3.3 V that the TC399's TTL-mode pads read reliably.

The BMP581 die itself is a 1.7–3.6 V part. A breakout with an onboard LDO
tolerates 5 V on VIN; a bare one does not. **Check whether yours has a
regulator before applying 5 V** — the BMP581 is not 5 V tolerant.

---

## 3. ⚠️ CS must be tied high, or the part answers on SPI, not I2C

The BMP581 selects its interface from the CS pin: **CS high = I2C, CS low =
SPI**. Some breakouts strap it high for you, some do not, and a floating CS is
the classic reason a correctly wired sensor never ACKs.

On the BMP388 this bit us in the same way — its CS had to go to 3V3, and pulling
it low latched SPI mode until a power cycle. Assume the BMP581 behaves the same
way and tie CS to +3V3 explicitly unless the breakout documents otherwise.

---

## 4. Address — 0x46 or 0x47

| SDO | 7-bit address |
|---|---|
| GND (or left open on boards with a pull-down) | **`0x46`** ← driver default |
| +3V3 | `0x47` |

`BMP581_I2C_ADDR` in `Bmp581.h` is `0x46`. If the bring-up read fails, try
`0x47` before suspecting anything else — it is one `#define` and costs nothing.

Note this is a **different address from the BMP388** (`0x77`), so nothing
carries over from the old wiring, and there is no collision with the planned
MMC5983MA (`0x30`) or NEO-M9N (`0x42`).

---

## 5. ⚠️ Pull-ups: the BMP581 is now the only device on the bus

This is the thing most likely to cost time tomorrow.

Until today the bus was held up by **two** breakouts in parallel — the
CJMCU-388's ~10 kΩ and the GY-521's ~4.7 kΩ, about **3.2 kΩ** combined. Removing
both removes **all** of it. Whatever the BMP581 breakout carries is now the
entire bus.

- Breakout **has** pull-ups (usually 4.7–10 kΩ): nothing to do.
- Breakout has **none**: the bus never idles high and **nothing will ACK** —
  which looks exactly like a dead sensor. Add **4.7 kΩ from SCL and SDA to
  +3V3** externally.

Check before powering up: with the board off, measure SCL→VDD and SDA→VDD on the
breakout. Open circuit means no pull-ups. Or after power-up, both lines must sit
at ~3.3 V idle — the firmware prints exactly this at boot:

```
I2C0 bus idle (SCL+SDA released)      <- good
I2C0 bus HELD - a slave is pulling SCL/SDA low
```

## 6. Firmware — what to change

The build currently initialises the BMP388 and the MPU-6050. With both removed
they will report `NO_RESPONSE` and be probed once a second forever. Harmless,
but the diagnostics will be permanently red, so swap them properly:

1. `Cpu0_Main.c` — replace `Bmp388_init()`/`Bmp388_read()` with the `Bmp581_*`
   equivalents; drop `Task_Imu`, `Ahrs_*` and the IMU `PeriphDiag_report()`
   until the new IMU arrives.
2. `PeriphDiag.h` — the `PERIPH_DIAG_BARO` slot serves the BMP581 unchanged;
   leave `PERIPH_DIAG_IMU` unused or remove it so its bits stop asserting.
3. `Bmp581.c/h` are **untracked** (unused code fails the MISRA gate: 8.7 plus a
   `s_present` collision with `Bmp388.c`). Once it has a caller that resolves
   itself — rename its statics `s_bmp581*` while you are there.
4. `docs/AurixTricore.a2l` — the `Baro*` measurements keep their addresses; only
   the descriptions need the sensor name changed.

## 7. Bring-up order — do these one at a time

Today's lesson was that skipping straight to "read the data" hides which layer
is broken. In order:

1. **Bus idle.** Boot and look for `I2C0 bus idle` on the UART. If it says HELD,
   stop — that is §5 or a short, and nothing else will work.
2. **CHIP_ID.** `Bmp581_readChipId()` should return **`0x50`**. This one
   transaction proves pins, pads, pull-ups, address, CS strap and driver
   together. ⚠️ **Verify the expected value against the datasheet** — the driver
   asserts `0x50`, which is also the BMP388's ID, so it may simply have been
   copied. A wrong constant here fails a perfectly good sensor.
3. **Raw registers.** Dump the measurement block before trusting the compensated
   output. Register addresses in `Bmp581.c` (`0x1D` data, `0x36` OSR, `0x37` ODR,
   `0x7E` CMD) are all `TODO(hw)` and unconfirmed.
4. **Plausibility.** ~954 hPa and ~484 m in Munich, matching what the BMP388
   read today — a direct cross-check against a known-good measurement.
5. **Liveness.** Confirm the value *changes*. `PeriphDiag` raises
   `BARO_STUCK_DATA` after 5 s of a frozen reading; that check exists precisely
   because a sensor that ACKs everything while its registers stand still cost
   two days and two wrongly-condemned chips.

## 8. Datasheet

Not in the repo. The BMP581 register map differs from the BMP388 — notably it
returns **ready-scaled** pressure and temperature, so there is **no 21-byte NVM
trim block and no Bosch compensation polynomial** to port. That makes the driver
much shorter than `Bmp388.c`, and it is why the existing `Bmp581.c` is ~110
lines against the BMP388's ~300.
