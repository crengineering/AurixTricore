# ICM42688P.md — TDK-InvenSense ICM-42688-P IMU, wiring & bring-up

Wiring and bring-up notes for the **ICM-42688-P** 6-axis IMU (3-axis gyro +
3-axis accel) on **QSPI0**, the flight IMU that replaces the interim MPU-6050.

**Board: TDK-InvenSense EV_ICM-42688-P evaluation board** (`Agile PN 860-80057`,
Rev B). Board-side source: **AN-000488 Rev 1.2**, the EVB user guide, in the
Quadrocopter repo as `doc/IMU.pdf`.

> **Pin-allocation SSoT:** the authoritative map is **`PINNING.md` §2.2**
> (QSPI0 pins) and **§2.5** (supply and level handling). **§2.5 owns the
> electrical plan** — this file expands on the evaluation-board side and must
> not contradict it.

Driver: `src/bsw/Icm42688.{c,h}` + QSPI0 bus service `src/bsw/Spi.{c,h}`,
firmware **v1.16.7**.

> ✅ **HARDWARE-VALIDATED 2026-08-01.** `WHO_AM_I` = `0x47`, `|a|` = 0.998 g at
> rest (gravity — orientation-independent, so it confirms the ±16 g scaling and
> the big-endian 16-bit assembly), gyro within hundredths of a deg/s, die
> temperature 27.1 °C matching the barometer, AHRS `RUNNING`, no `IMU_*`
> diagnostics bits.
> ⚠️ **SCLK is on P20.13, not P22.8** — P22.7/P22.8 are unusable on this
> TriBoard (`PINNING.md` §2.2).

## 1. Supply — 3.3 V, injected past the on-board LDOs

The EVB carries two LDOs (`U2` XC6210B302MR-G → 3.0 V, `U3` TLV70218DBVT →
1.8 V) and two jumpers that select which one reaches the die. AN-000488 §2
therefore says *"VDDIO and VDD can be selected between 1.8V and 3.0V"* — but
**that is a limitation of the evaluation board, not of the ICM-42688-P**, which
accepts VDD and VDDIO anywhere in 1.71–3.6 V.

So the plan (`PINNING.md` §2.5) **bypasses the LDOs and runs the die at 3.3 V**,
putting the IMU in the same domain as every other sensor on this build:

| Inject | Into | Net driven |
|---|---|---|
| **+3V3** | **JP2 pin 2** | `VDD_EVB` |
| **+3V3** | **JP1 pin 2** | `VDDIO_EVB` |

**⚠️ Leave BOTH jumpers OPEN.** Confirmed against the schematic:

```
JP2:  pin1 = 3V0_EVB    pin2 = VDD_EVB      pin3 = 1V8_EVB
JP1:  pin1 = VDD_EVB    pin2 = VDDIO_EVB    pin3 = 1V8_EVB
```

With the jumpers open, the 3.0 V and 1.8 V LDO outputs are disconnected and
cannot fight the injected 3.3 V. **Fitting a jumper would tie an LDO output to
the 3.3 V rail and back-drive it** — and a jumper on JP2 1–2 would drag VDD down
to 3.0 V, which is exactly what this arrangement exists to avoid.

Take the 3.3 V from the regulated **+3V3** rail (X702·78/80 or X703·70) — **not**
`VCC_IN` (raw 3.5–40 V) and **not** `V_UC` (5 V).

### CN1 pin 19 is VIN — and is deliberately NOT used

Worth knowing so it is not wired by mistake, and because the app note gets it
wrong: **AN-000488 Table 2 omits pin 19 entirely.** It lists signals for pins
3, 4, 5, 6, 11, 13, 16, 18, 20 and declares 1, 2, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17 as
NC — 19 of 20 pins, with pin 19 in neither list. The schematic settles it: pin
19 carries the only remaining trace and it feeds both LDOs, so **CN1 pin 19 is
the EVB's supply input**.

Feeding it would work, but it routes power *through* the 3.0 V LDO and forces
the die to 3.0 V. **Leave pin 19 unconnected** and use the JP1/JP2 injection
above instead.

## 2. ⚠️ Level shifting — three lines, MCU → IMU

TC399 **Port 22 is in the VEXT = 5 V domain**. The ICM-42688-P's absolute
maximum on any I/O pin is **VDDIO + 0.3 V = 3.6 V** at our 3.3 V rail. Driving a
Port-22 output straight at it applies 5 V and destroys the part.

Per `PINNING.md` §2.5, on **SCLK, SDI (MOSI) and CS**:

```
   TC399 out ──[ 1.0 kΩ ]──┬────────── EVB input
                           │
                        [ 2.0 kΩ ]
                           │
                          GND
```

**Bench-verified at 3.30 V.** The EVB's own 10 kΩ pull-ups to VDDIO
(`R1`,`R2`,`R3`, on CS/SCLK/SDI) sit in parallel and barely move it:

| MCU drives | Node voltage | IMU threshold | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.0 V | **3.33 V** | VIH ≈ 0.7 × 3.3 = 2.31 V | fine — 0.03 V over the rail, far inside the 0.3 V allowance |
| 0 V | **0.21 V** | VIL ≈ 0.3 × 3.3 = 0.99 V | fine |

Fallback if a divider proves marginal at speed: **74LVC1T45** per line. (This
supersedes an earlier buffer-IC plan.)

**IMU → MCU** — these carry **no divider**. The IMU drives 3.3 V, which is
already safe for a 5 V pad; the problem is the other way round, since that pad's
default threshold is ≈3.5 V and would never see a high. The fix is in software:

```c
IfxPort_setPinPadDriver(&MODULE_P22, pin, IfxPort_PadDriver_ttlSpeed1);  /* VIH = 2.0 V */
```

Same technique `I2c.c` already uses for the 3.3 V sensor bus.

`SDO` (MISO) needs nothing else. **`INT1` gets a 10 kΩ pulldown** to GND
(`PINNING.md` §2.5):

```
   EVB CN1 pin 3 (INT1) ──┬────────── TC399 P22.7  (GPIO in, TTL pad)
                          │
                       [ 10 kΩ ]
                          │
                         GND
```

Note this is a **pulldown, not a divider** — there is no series resistor, so the
IMU's 3.3 V high arrives undivided. Its only job is to hold the line at a
defined low during the window between power-up and the IMU configuring INT1 as
an output; a floating input there can produce phantom edges.

⚠️ **The EVB does not provide this.** `R6`/`R7` on the board schematic are
marked NM (not mounted) and sit on the RESV pins anyway, so INT1 runs straight
from U1 pin 4 to CN1 pin 3 with nothing on it. **Measuring CN1 pin 3 → GND as
OL is therefore the correct reading for a bare board** — it means the pulldown
is not fitted yet, not that anything is broken. With the resistor in it reads
10 kΩ.

For contrast, on the MCU→IMU lines the same measurement reads **3.0 kΩ**
(1 kΩ + 2 kΩ in series to GND), which is how the two wiring styles are told
apart with an ohmmeter.

## 3. Wiring

`CN1` = HDR 10×2, male, right-angle, 2.54 mm.

| From | EVB signal | Dir | Wire to | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **JP2 pin 2** | `VDD` | power | **+3V3** | jumper OPEN, §1 |
| **JP1 pin 2** | `VDDIO` | power | **+3V3** | jumper OPEN, §1 |
| **CN1 11, 13** | GND | — | GND | either or both |
| **CN1 4** | `AP_CS` | MCU→IMU | **P22.11** (`SLSO10`) | ⚠️ 1k/2k divider |
| **CN1 16** | `AP_SCL/AP_SCLK` | MCU→IMU | **P20.13** (`SCLK`) | ⚠️ 1k/2k divider. **NOT P22.8** — unusable, see PINNING.md §2.2 |
| **CN1 18** | `AP_SDA/AP_SDIO` | MCU→IMU | **P22.10** (`MTSR`, MOSI) | ⚠️ 1k/2k divider |
| **CN1 20** | `AP_AD0/AP_SDO` | IMU→MCU | **P22.9** (`MRST`, MISO) | direct; TTL pad |
| CN1 3 | `INT1` | — | **leave unwired** | P22.7 is unusable; the driver polls at 50 Hz |
| CN1 5, 6 | `INT2/FSYNC` | — | leave open | |
| **CN1 19** | `VIN` | — | **leave open** | §1 — do not use |
| CN1 1,2,7,8,9,10,12,14,15,17 | NC | — | leave open | |

Wire by **pin name**, not header hole number — see `PINNING.md` §2.2 for why the
hole numbers there were removed. `INT1` is left unwired: P22.7 is unusable on
this board and the driver polls at 50 Hz, so the interrupt is not needed.

## 4. Datasheet — obtained, and it confirmed the driver

AN-000488 (the EVB guide) has **no register map**; the device datasheet
**DS-000347 rev 1.2** does, and it is in the Quadrocopter repo as
`doc/IMU_chip_datasheet.pdf`. It validated every assumption the driver had been
written on:

| Driver assumed | Datasheet |
|---|---|
| `WHO_AM_I` at 0x75 = 0x47 | §14.58 "Address: 117 (75h) … Reset value: 0x47" |
| R/W = MSB of byte 1, read = 1 | §9.6 item 5 |
| Burst reads supported | §9.6 item 7 |
| SPI mode 0 **or** 3 | `DEVICE_CONFIG` bit 4: *"0: Mode 0 and Mode 3 (default)"* |
| `DEVICE_CONFIG` 0x11, `TEMP_DATA1` 0x1D | Bank 0 register map |
| Soft-reset settle | *"wait 1 ms … before any other register access"* |
| Input thresholds | **VIH = 0.7 × VDDIO**, VIL = 0.3 × VDDIO |

Two behaviours worth knowing, both seen on hardware:
- **Gyro reads `0x8000` (−32768) until its first sample.** The datasheet lists
  this as the power-on-reset value, and gyro start-up is 30 ms — so the boot
  dump legitimately shows it while the accelerometer (10 ms) is already valid.
- **`0x00` on every register means no data is returning at all.** With CS
  deasserted the part holds SDO high-impedance and the EVB's 100 kΩ R5 pulls it
  low, so a missing clock or a missing MISO looks identical to a dead part.

## 5. Bring-up order

1. **Jumpers OFF, then meter the rails.** With +3V3 injected at JP1 pin 2 and
   JP2 pin 2 and both jumpers removed, both pins must read **3.3 V**. If either
   reads 3.0 V or 1.8 V, a jumper is still fitted and an LDO is driving the net.
2. **Meter the dividers before connecting them.** Drive each MCU pin high in
   isolation and confirm ≈3.3 V at the EVB end. This is the step that protects a
   part whose absolute maximum is 3.6 V from a 5 V port.
3. **`WHO_AM_I`.** Should read **`0x47`**. That single transfer proves pins, pad
   modes, level shifting, CS, SPI mode and driver together.
4. **Boot dump** — configuration registers plus the raw data block.
5. **Plausibility.** At rest |a| ≈ 1 g on one axis and ≈0 on the others, gyro ≈0
   on all three. Rotating the board must move the expected axis.
6. **Liveness.** `PeriphDiag` raises `STUCK_DATA` after 5 s of a frozen value.

## 6. Build gotcha — the iLLD QSPI tree is excluded

`.cproject` currently excludes the whole QSPI source tree:
`Libraries/iLLD/TC3xx/Tricore/Qspi`, `Qspi/Std`, `Qspi/SpiMaster`,
`Qspi/SpiSlave`. **They must be un-excluded**, exactly as the `I2c` tree was for
the sensor bus, or `IfxQspi_SpiMaster_*` will not link.

Expect the same follow-on as the I2C bring-up: root-relative iLLD include paths,
and the ADS `tc3-hardware.json` cache needing a nudge.
